title: A Roadmap for Understanding LLMs
---

## The path

```
Step 1: intuition for neural networks and deep learning
    ↓
Step 2: the Transformer architecture (the core of the core)
    ↓
Step 3: how a large model is trained (pretraining → SFT → RLHF)
    ↓
Step 4: key inference-time concepts (how to use a model well)
    ↓
Step 5: how agents and tool calling work (tying it to what you already do)
```

---

## Step 1: intuition for neural networks and deep learning (1–2 weeks)

You do not need to derive the maths. The point is intuition:

- What a neural network is actually learning — parameters, weights, loss functions, gradient descent. Understand *what they are*, not how to derive them.
- What representation learning (embeddings) means — why text can become a vector, and why distance between vectors can stand for semantic similarity.

**Resources:**

- [3Blue1Brown — the neural networks playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZZWrBYkx7Otcjr3eCLZDCgfpqnxMY29s) (visual explanations, no mathematical pressure, widely considered the best introduction)
- [But what is a Neural Network? | Deep Learning, Chapter 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk) (the first episode, linked separately so you can just start)

---

## Step 2: the Transformer architecture (spend the time here)

This is the foundation of every large model. Understand it and the rest follows:

- **Attention** — why it solves long-range dependency, and what "attention" is actually attending to
- **Self-attention and multi-head attention** — you do not need to derive the matrix operations, but you do need the intuition of "which other tokens is each token looking at"
- **Why Transformers can be trained in parallel** — the advantage over RNNs

**Resource:**

- [The Illustrated Transformer — Jay Alammar](https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/) (the canonical introduction, cited in courses at MIT, Stanford, Harvard and elsewhere; no mathematical pressure)

---

## Step 3: how a large model is trained

Understanding how the model came to exist matters a lot for product judgement:

- **Pretraining** — predicting the next word over an enormous corpus, and what the model learns from it
- **SFT (supervised fine-tuning)** — how you make a model follow instructions
- **RLHF / RLAIF** — how human feedback shapes output towards human preference, and why this step is what makes a model actually usable
- **Scaling laws** — why "bigger model plus more data" is usually stronger, and where that stops holding

**Resources:**

- [Illustrating Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) — Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/rlhf) (the most-cited explainer on the subject; walks through all three training stages with diagrams)
- [Anthropic's Core Views on AI Safety](https://www.anthropic.com/research/core-views-on-ai-safety) (Anthropic's own view of where RLHF and other alignment techniques sit in training; safety-oriented, useful as background)

---

## Step 4: inference-time concepts (these decide how well you use a model)

- **Context window** — why long text gets "forgotten", and how tokens are counted
- **Temperature and top-p sampling** — why the same prompt does not give the same answer twice
- **Why hallucination happens** — understood from next-word prediction, not treated as a mystery
- **How RAG works and where it stops** — approached from "why do we need RAG at all", alongside the agent work you have already done

(No single resource listed here. Work it out from what you observe in practice, reasoning back from the first two steps.)

---

## Step 5: what is behind agents and tool calling (connects to what you already build)

- How tool use is actually implemented: the model emits structured JSON, something external runs it, and the result goes back into the context
- Why multi-turn agent loops drift off course — context management and error accumulation
- Tie it back to LangGraph: why state persistence and human intervention are necessary at the mechanism level

---

## Suggested pace

1. Read **The Illustrated Transformer** first (1–2 days; the highest return of anything here)
2. If it is heavy going, go back and do the **3Blue1Brown** basics
3. Then read the **RLHF explainer** for the training pipeline. This is what decides whether you can explain "why does the model sometimes make things up" and "why do different models have such different styles"
4. Finally connect it to the agent work you already do, and it clicks
