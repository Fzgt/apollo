(48 minutes, the whole drive back from my hometown on the motorway.)

1. Give me a short introduction. — He cut in early: skip Baidu, I spent six years in LBS and I know your manager well.
2. Let me introduce myself too. I run the front-end organisation at Alipay. The team is split across a lot of areas — which one interests you?
3. Talk about where front end has gone in the last year and where it is heading. How do you keep up? Then summarise the core pain points of in-app front end, and how you would solve them.
4. You have covered a lot — performance, tooling, infrastructure. Let's take a few of those words one at a time.
5. You mentioned JIT. What is the essential difference between an internal DSL and an external DSL? If you were designing one, what would you weigh — syntactic noise, for example?
6. Then how would you design the interpreter and the compiler for an internal DSL? Which language do you know best here?
7. We just touched on C and LISP. Can you explain functional programming as you understand it? Is it a mathematical question?
8. You have said a lot, including SKI combinators. Going deeper: what is the fundamental difference between C and LISP at the very bottom?
9. You also mentioned web AI, low-code, no-code. Can you walk through the core concepts — weights, layers, logistic regression, underfitting and overfitting?
10. All right, your AI is not deep. Let's take a practical problem instead. Say you have time to learn TensorFlow.js, and the team's goal this year is: someone hands you TSX code, and you have to work out what kind of component it is and generate code from it. Images are easy — CNN. What about code? Is an AST enough?
11. Your instinct is right. Enough on AI. Have you ever studied it systematically?
12. AI gets a lot of hype. What is its most fundamental goal? And what do you think is the biggest trap in TensorFlow.js compared with TensorFlow?
13. Back to in-app, since we are Alipay. What is the future for an in-app front-end engineer? If you ran this team, what would you do for it? What technical forms does in-app H5 take, and what is the most urgent problem?
14. Why did you start a company? Do you have a product? How do you split your time? I see you used serverless — if you were building a serverless product from scratch, how would you do it?
15. Finally: what is "large front end" to you? What actually counts as technical depth? What does the end state of a front-end engineer's career look like? And what would you want to work on at Alipay?
16. Our process is long — probably six to eight rounds. Is that all right with you?

End of interview, back to driving.

● Avoiding lifecycle traps comes down to two things:

1. Don't call code that should not be called at that moment.
2. Don't forget to call the code that should be called — the performance work, the unbinding, the cleanup.

● Seven common traps:

1. `getDerivedStateFromProps` invites an anti-pattern that blurs the line between controlled and uncontrolled components.
2. `componentWillMount` is deprecated, mainly because the new async architecture can call it more than once. Put network requests in `componentDidMount`.
3. `componentWillReceiveProps` is deprecated and replaced by `getDerivedStateFromProps`, for the same reason as `componentWillMount`: under async rendering it can be called more than once, and it invites side effects and derived-state anti-patterns (the reason given in React's 2018 "Update on Async Rendering" post — not performance).
4. `shouldComponentUpdate` returns true or false to decide whether to re-render; it is a performance tool.
5. `componentWillUpdate` is deprecated; rewrite it using `getSnapshotBeforeUpdate` together with `componentDidUpdate`.
6. Forgetting to unbind events or clear timers in `componentWillUnmount` is an easy source of bugs.
7. Without an error boundary catching through `componentDidCatch`, the user gets a blank, dead screen.
